title: Ternary flash with Antoine vapour pressures: bubble point, dew point and the half-vaporized temperature
parts: a,b,c,d,e
---
A liquid feed contains benzene (1), toluene (2) and o-xylene (3) with mass fractions w₁ = 0.30, w₂ = 0.40 and w₃ = 0.30. The molar masses are M₁ = 78.11, M₂ = 92.14 and M₃ = 106.17 g/mol. The mixture is flashed at a constant total pressure P = 120 kPa. Assume ideal behaviour in both phases (Raoult's law, ideal vapour), with K_i = P_i^sat/P and a single vapour and a single liquid phase at equilibrium.

The vapour pressures follow the Antoine equation log₁₀ P_i^sat = A_i − B_i/(C_i + T), with P^sat in mmHg and T in °C (logarithm to base 10; 1 mmHg = 0.133322 kPa):

| Component | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| benzene | 6.90565 | 1211.033 | 220.790 |
| toluene | 6.95464 | 1344.800 | 219.482 |
| o-xylene | 6.99891 | 1474.679 | 213.686 |

Give the temperatures in °C and the other results as dimensionless mole fractions, with at least 4 significant figures.

(a) Calculate the bubble-point temperature of the feed at 120 kPa, in °C.
(b) Calculate the dew-point temperature of the feed at 120 kPa, in °C.
(c) The feed is flashed isothermally at 112 °C and 120 kPa. Calculate the vapour fraction V/F (moles of vapour per mole of feed).
(d) For the flash in (c), calculate the mole fraction of benzene in the vapour leaving the flash.
(e) Calculate the flash temperature, in °C, at which exactly half of the feed (V/F = 0.5, on a molar basis) is vaporized at 120 kPa.
