# ASD-STE100 Rules (condensed, for the 100% dial setting)

Simplified Technical English: a controlled language for technical writing. These are the rules to enforce at `p = 100`.

## Vocabulary
- Use words with their approved meaning and approved part of speech only. When a word has several meanings, use only the approved one.
- Prefer short, single-word verbs. Never use phrasal verbs: write "do", not "carry out"; "increase", not "round up"; "use", not "make use of".
- Project-specific technical terms are allowed as Technical Names: nouns only, spelled identically every time.
- Do not use "etc.", "e.g.", or "i.e."

## Sentences
- Procedural sentences: maximum 20 words. Descriptive sentences: maximum 25 words.
- One topic per sentence. One command per sentence in procedures.
- Active voice. Imperative mood for procedural steps ("Draw the line", not "The line should be drawn").
- No verb + -ing used as a noun ("design" as a noun is fine; "designing the column" as a noun phrase is not).
- Avoid pronouns. Repeat the noun instead of "it/they". "This/that/these" are allowed only as adjectives directly before a noun ("this equation", never standalone "this").

## Modality and numbers
- "Must" expresses a requirement. "Can" expresses ability. Do not use "should" for requirements.
- Write numbers as numerals. Use identical wording for the same concept throughout.

## Definitions
- Define every symbol, letter, and technical term at or before its first use, with SI units where applicable. A definitions section before the procedure is the normal way to do this.
- Define even "obvious" symbols (x, y, R, q) — no exceptions.

## Structure
- Purpose, Definitions, Conditions/assumptions, numbered procedural Steps, Notes.
- In step-off/counting procedures, state the start point, the repeated move, the stop condition, and what to record.
