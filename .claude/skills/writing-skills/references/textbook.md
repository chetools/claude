# Textbook forms [load for detail sentences]

These forms govern detail sentences: a sentence whose job is to let the reader reproduce or verify a step (a definition, a derivation step, an assumption, a procedure step, a caveat, or a worked calculation). A sentence that states what was done, found, or decided follows the core or `business.md`. A sentence that would do both is split: the derivation goes into its own detail sentence, and the result sentence keeps the frame form (core rule 14). Where a detail sentence's form here differs from a core rule, the form here governs that sentence.

## Forms

T1. A detail sentence carries its object's full name (the law, equation, quantity, or step it is about), even mid-document.
T2. A name is fixed at first use with "is defined as", "is called", or "is known as"; a name the field already uses takes "is referred to as", with its source or reason where one is given. A mathematical symbol is fixed with "denotes" or "is denoted by". After a displayed equation, the "where" roster is the default definition site for its symbols. Standard notation (core rule 4) takes no fixing.
T3. A derivation step is marked at the step: "thus" where the premises stand in the sentences just before, "therefore" as a variant, "hence" rarely. A because-clause attaches the ground to the step, result, or comparison it supports.
T4. A step whose source is not the preceding line opens with its source: "From Eq. (6), …"; "By an overall material balance, …". A criterion found by setting two relations side by side is stated as a comparison: "Comparison of Eqs. (2) and (5) shows that …".
T5. A transformation takes the equation as subject: "becomes" (substitution, limiting case), "reduces to" (special case under a stated condition), "simplifies to", "can be written", "rearranges to".
T6. The operation verb matches the route: determine (balance, criterion, specification), calculate (numerical evaluation), obtain from (data source, table, earlier result), find from (relation or data set), estimate (judgement). Equations and procedures "give", "yield", or "show"; methods and designs "provide". The same verb repeats for the same route; these verbs are exempt from the one-name rule.
T7. A worked example states its task as an imperative or question carrying every datum, uses an imperative at each choice point ("Assume a pressure of 74 psia"; "Take 1 g as a basis"), and states each assumption before the first equation that uses it. Where an expected limiting behaviour selects the law, the expectation is stated first and checked at the close.
T8. A worked example closes with a check: the result against experiment, an estimate, a predecessor, or a rival method, with the difference stated, or the idealizations named with the direction of error each causes. The closing sentence turns the number into the statement the example exists to make, without an inference marker (core rule 14).
T9. Change takes its matching frame. Covariation: "As X increases, Y increases, but Z decreases", one change verb per variable, closed by a trade-off sentence where the pair sets one. Imposed step: "changes from X to Y". Range: "varies from X to Y". A named producer takes the transitive frame (a section reduces a quantity). "Drop" is a noun.
T10. An omitted effect is named with its ground ("can be neglected because …", or "negligible" in running prose); see also core rule 32. An approximation's adequacy is stated as fitness for a named purpose or as a number, never as a bare grade.
T11. A grading word ("important", "significant", "acceptable", "reasonable") stands only with its ground (what it limits or decides, a comparison, a practice statement, or the number) in the same sentence or the one before. A general claim carries "usually" or "typically", attached to its number, range, or configuration.
T12. "Note that" heads a sign convention, a validity caveat, or a hidden assumption, with the substance in the same sentence, at the point of risk.
T13. A chapter or section closes with a restatement of its content, with no new derivation, no new claim, and no task imperatives.

## Pitfalls

- "Thus" with no premise in the sentences just before it.
- "Note that" followed by nothing checkable (a condition, a sign, or a consequence).
- One-name discipline applied to the operation verbs of T6, which vary by route.
- A change verb that invents a cause where no producer is named.
- Field terms read as grading words in a mechanical scan: "key component" and "pressure drop" are terminology.
- Splits the textbooks do not settle: "compared with" against "compared to", and "with respect to" against "in terms of"; one form is held per document. Solution tense is past for a simulator run narrated as an event, present elsewhere.

## Completion check

Every derivation step is marked by a connective or opened by its source; operation verbs follow the route; assumptions stand before the first equation that uses them; every grading word has its ground; no frame sentence carries an imperative, a step connective, or a where roster.
