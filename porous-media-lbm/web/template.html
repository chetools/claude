Packed bed on the GPU: flow, permeability & RTD

Fluid flows through a bed of particles, solved at the pore scale with the lattice-Boltzmann method. Each cell of the grid is updated in parallel by your graphics card (WebGPU), so the flow is computed live: millions of cell updates per frame. From the simulated flow you can measure the permeability and compare it with Kozeny–Carman and Ergun, see channeling, and release a tracer pulse to obtain the residence-time distribution and the axial dispersion coefficient.

Flow is from left to right, driven by a uniform body force that stands in for the pressure gradient. The domain is periodic, so particles leaving one edge re-enter at the opposite one. Most of the flow goes through a few connected wide pores: this is channeling. Switch the view to vorticity to see the shear layers around the particles. Use Mouse: add particles to block a channel and watch the permeability drop. The colours are scaled to the current maximum (gain ×1).

Once the flow is steady, press Inject tracer pulse on the Flow field tab. A square pulse of tracer (width set in the sidebar) enters at the inlet, x = 0. Two detectors 2 d from either end record the flow-weighted (cup-mixing) concentration. The analysis below uses the difference between the two detectors, so it does not depend on what happens at the inlet and outlet (Theory §9). Ticking Freeze flow runs only the tracer, which is about twice as fast once the flow is steady.

No pulse yet.

Try raising Pem (slower molecular diffusion): DL/Dm grows almost in proportion. This is mechanical dispersion, where streams that take different paths through the bed spread the pulse regardless of molecular diffusion. In the empty-channel preset, compare the result with Taylor–Aris dispersion.

Permeability vs porosity

Builds a series of beds with the current layout, seed and grid, runs each to steady creeping flow (Rep = 0.05, τ = 1.5) and measures k. The Kozeny constant fitted to all beds shows how far the capillary-bundle picture holds.

From Darcy to Ergun: the Reynolds sweep

The current bed is driven harder and harder. To keep the lattice Mach number low, the viscosity is lowered at high Re, which is allowed because with TRT the steady flow does not depend on τ (Theory §5). The friction factor is compared with Ergun's equation.

Runs the same case twice: once with the GPU shaders (in 32-bit floats) and once with lbm.py in Python (numpy, 64-bit floats, running in your browser). The case uses the current sidebar bed on a coarse grid of about 12k cells, 600 flow steps from rest, then 400 steps of a tracer pulse. The bed comes from the same random seed in both languages, so the masks must be identical cell by cell. The fields should then agree to float32 round-off.
